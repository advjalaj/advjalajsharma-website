Criminal law · Article 22 · BNSS

Detained by the police unlawfully? What to do

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 4 min read

In short

  1. Grounds of arrest must be given in writing, in a language the person understands.
  2. The person must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours.
  3. Keeping someone at a police station without recording an arrest is illegal detention.
  4. Remedies include release by the court, a habeas corpus petition and compensation.

1. What is the legal problem?

A person has been picked up by the police and is being held at a police station: no arrest has been recorded, no reason has been given in writing, the family has not been told, or 24 hours have passed without production before a Magistrate.

2. What does the law say?

Article 22 of the Constitution and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 give every arrested person these rights:

  • Grounds of arrest in writing. The grounds must be given in writing, in a language the person understands, for every offence under any law. In a rare urgent situation they may first be told orally, but the written grounds must be supplied at least two hours before the person is produced for remand.
  • Arrest memo. The officer must prepare a memo of arrest, attested by a witness, and must bear a clear name and identification.
  • Information to family. A relative or friend named by the person must be told of the arrest and the place of custody.
  • Production within 24 hours. The person must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding travel time.
  • Advocate. The person may meet an advocate of their choice during interrogation, though not throughout it.
  • Medical examination on arrest.
  • Women are not ordinarily arrested after sunset and before sunrise, and only by or in the presence of a woman officer.
  • For offences punishable with up to seven years, the police must generally issue a notice of appearance instead of arresting, unless arrest is necessary for reasons recorded in writing.

Consequences. If the written grounds are not supplied, the arrest and the remand are illegal and the person must be released. This release is not bail; it is release from unlawful custody, and a later chargesheet does not cure the illegality. Re-arrest is possible only after the grounds are supplied and the Magistrate permits it. The officer responsible may face departmental action, and courts can award compensation for unlawful detention.

There is no lawful “detention for questioning” outside an arrest. Holding a person at a police station against their will without recording an arrest is unlawful.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Find out which police station is holding the person and note the time they were taken.
  • Ask the officer for the grounds of arrest and the arrest memo.
  • Send a written representation by email to the Superintendent of Police and the District Magistrate, stating the time of detention and asking for the person’s production or release.
  • In the same letter, ask the police to preserve the CCTV footage of the police station.
  • If the person is not produced or released, a habeas corpus petition can be filed before the High Court, which hears such petitions urgently.
  • The detained person should not sign blank or unread papers.
  • After release, get a medical examination done if there are any injuries.

4. What is the limitation period?

  • Habeas corpus: no limitation period; it must be filed while the detention continues.
  • Human Rights Commission: a complaint should be made within one year of the incident.
  • Civil suit for compensation for false imprisonment: within one year from the end of the imprisonment.
  • A complaint to the Police Complaints Authority should be made promptly.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • The arrest memo, notice and any document given by the police
  • A note of the time and place of detention and the names of officers
  • Call logs and messages showing when the person was taken
  • Names and phone numbers of witnesses
  • Copies of representations sent and the request to preserve CCTV footage
  • Medical reports

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

CCTV footage may be overwritten, the Human Rights Commission may decline to enquire into an incident more than a year old, and a suit for compensation may become time-barred. The constitutional remedy of compensation remains available, but delay makes the facts harder to prove.

Common questions

Can the police keep me at the station without arresting me?

No. If you are not free to leave, you are in custody, and the safeguards of arrest apply.

Is it enough if the grounds of arrest are told orally?

No. They must also be given in writing, in a language the person understands.

Who can file a habeas corpus petition?

Any relative or friend of the detained person can file it on their behalf.
