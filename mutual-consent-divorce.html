Family law · Section 13B Hindu Marriage Act

Divorce by mutual consent: the process and the waiting period

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  1. You must have lived separately for at least one year.
  2. Settle alimony, custody and belongings in writing before filing.
  3. File a joint petition (first motion); the second motion follows after 6 months.
  4. The court can waive the six-month wait in suitable cases.

1. What is the legal problem?

Both of you agree that the marriage has ended and want a divorce without contested litigation.

2. What does the law say?

For Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists, Section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 allows divorce by mutual consent. Marriages under the Special Marriage Act have a similar provision.

  • You must have been living separately for at least one year and agree that you cannot live together.
  • You file a joint petition in the Family Court (the first motion).
  • The second motion is made not earlier than 6 months and not later than 18 months after the petition, after which the court grants the decree.
  • The Supreme Court has held that the six-month period can be waived where the parties have settled everything and reconciliation is not possible.
  • Either spouse may withdraw consent before the decree is passed.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Agree on alimony or maintenance, usually as a one-time amount.
  • Agree on custody and visiting rights for any children.
  • Agree on the return of jewellery and belongings, and on any jointly held property.
  • Agree on what happens to any other pending cases between you.
  • Put all of this in a written settlement signed by both of you.

4. What is the limitation period?

There is no limitation period for filing. The time rules are: one year of separation before filing, and the second motion between 6 and 18 months after the petition is filed.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • Proof of marriage: marriage certificate, photographs or invitation card
  • Identity and address proof of both spouses
  • Proof of separate residence for the past year
  • The signed settlement agreement
  • Income details and details of any children
  • A list of jewellery and belongings to be returned

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

If the second motion is not made within 18 months, the court may treat the petition as not pursued, and you may have to file afresh. If one spouse withdraws consent before the decree, the court cannot grant divorce on that petition.

Common questions

Do both spouses have to attend court?

Both spouses generally appear at the first and second motions; courts may allow appearance by video in suitable cases.

Can we skip the six-month waiting period?

You can ask the court to waive it. The court considers whether you have lived apart long enough, whether mediation has failed and whether all issues are settled.

This guide is general information on the law as it stands in November 2026. It is not legal advice; time limits and outcomes depend on the facts of each case.
