Anticipatory bail: what to do if you fear arrest

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  1. Get a copy of the FIR or complaint as soon as possible.
  2. Apply to the Sessions Court or High Court before any arrest.
  3. Do not contact the complainant or witnesses.
  4. Once arrested, anticipatory bail is no longer available; regular bail must be sought.

1. What is the legal problem?

An FIR or complaint has been filed against you, or you have reason to believe one will be, for a non-bailable offence, and you fear that the police may arrest you.

2. What does the law say?

Under Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (earlier Section 438 CrPC), the Sessions Court or the High Court can direct that, if you are arrested, you be released on bail.

  • The court looks at the nature and seriousness of the accusation, your past record, the risk of you fleeing, and whether the case appears to have been filed to harass you.
  • Bail usually comes with conditions: join the investigation when called, do not leave India without permission, and do not threaten or influence witnesses.
  • It is not available for certain serious sexual offences against minors, and some special laws restrict it.
  • For offences punishable with up to seven years, the police are generally required to issue a notice of appearance instead of arresting straight away, if the conditions for arrest are not met.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Obtain a copy of the FIR. In most cases it is available on the state police website within 24 hours.
  • If you receive a police notice, keep it and note the date and time it asks you to appear.
  • Collect documents that support your version of events.
  • Do not contact the complainant or witnesses about the case.

4. What is the limitation period?

There is no fixed limitation period. The application must be made before arrest, as soon as you reasonably fear it. Courts generally expect you to apply promptly once the FIR is known.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • Copy of the FIR or complaint
  • Any notice from the police
  • Documents supporting your version: agreements, payment records, messages, emails
  • Identity and address proof
  • Details of any other case pending against you

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

If you are arrested before applying, anticipatory bail can no longer be granted. You will have to apply for regular bail, usually after being produced before the Magistrate, which may mean some time in custody.

Common questions

Is anticipatory bail granted for a fixed period?

Ordinarily the Supreme Court has held that protection need not be limited to a fixed period, though a court may impose a time limit or conditions in a particular case.

Can I get interim protection while the application is heard?

Courts often grant interim protection from arrest until the next date of hearing, depending on the facts.

This guide is general information on the law as it stands in November 2026. It is not legal advice; time limits and outcomes depend on the facts of each case.
