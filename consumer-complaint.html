Consumer law · Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Filing a consumer complaint: defective goods, poor service or builder delay

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  1. Complain in writing to the seller or service provider first and keep a copy.
  2. File before the District, State or National Commission depending on the amount paid.
  3. You can file where you live or work.
  4. File within two years of the problem arising.

1. What is the legal problem?

You paid for goods or a service and received something defective, a service that was deficient, or you were misled by an unfair trade practice. A common example is a builder who fails to hand over a flat on time.

2. What does the law say?

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 lets you file a complaint before a Consumer Commission for replacement, refund, repair or compensation. Which Commission depends on the amount you paid:

CommissionAmount paid for the goods or service
District CommissionUp to ₹50 lakh
State CommissionAbove ₹50 lakh and up to ₹2 crore
National CommissionAbove ₹2 crore

You may file where you live or work, and complaints can also be filed online.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Write to the seller, service provider or builder describing the problem and keep a copy.
  • Note dates, reference numbers and the names of people you spoke to.
  • Take photographs of a defective product and do not discard it.
  • A legal notice before filing is not compulsory, but it puts your demand clearly on record.

4. What is the limitation period?

A complaint must be filed within two years from the date the cause of action arose. A Commission can admit a late complaint if it is satisfied there was sufficient cause for the delay.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • Invoice, bill or payment receipts
  • Warranty card, agreement, allotment letter or builder–buyer agreement
  • All correspondence: emails, letters, chats, complaint numbers
  • Photographs, and any inspection or expert report on the defect

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

A complaint filed after two years is likely to be dismissed unless the delay is explained and condoned. Depending on the facts, a civil suit may still be possible within its own limitation period, but it is usually slower than the consumer route.

Common questions

Do I need a lawyer to file a consumer complaint?

No. You can file and argue it yourself, though many people choose to be represented.

Is there a fee for filing?

Fees depend on the amount claimed and are modest; for small claims the fee is nil.

This guide is general information on the law as it stands in November 2026. It is not legal advice; time limits and outcomes depend on the facts of each case.
