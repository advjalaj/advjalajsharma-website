Cheque bounce · Section 138 NI Act

Cheque bounced? What to do under Section 138 of the NI Act

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  1. Keep the original cheque and the bank’s return memo.
  2. Send a legal notice within 30 days of learning the cheque bounced.
  3. The drawer then has 15 days to pay.
  4. If unpaid, file the complaint within one month after those 15 days.

1. What is the legal problem?

A cheque given to you to pay a debt has come back unpaid. Banks call this a cheque being dishonoured; most people say it bounced. The usual reasons are “funds insufficient” or “exceeds arrangement”.

2. What does the law say?

Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, it is a criminal offence when a cheque given for a debt bounces and the person who issued it does not pay within 15 days of receiving your written notice.

  • Punishment: up to two years’ imprisonment, a fine of up to twice the cheque amount, or both.
  • While the case is pending, the court can order interim compensation of up to 20% of the cheque amount.
  • The case can be settled at any stage.
  • A cheque given as “security” can also lead to a case if the debt was actually due when it was presented.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Keep the original cheque and the return memo. Do not hand the cheque back.
  • Send a written demand notice stating the cheque details and asking for payment within 15 days.
  • Send it by Speed Post or registered post to the correct address and keep the receipt and tracking record.

4. What is the limitation period?

StepTime limit
Present the chequeWithin 3 months of the date on the cheque
Send the legal noticeWithin 30 days of learning it bounced
Drawer paysWithin 15 days of receiving the notice
File the complaintWithin 1 month after the 15 days end

The complaint is filed before the Magistrate where your own bank branch is located, not where the drawer lives.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • The original cheque and the bank’s return memo
  • A copy of the legal notice, the postal receipt and the tracking report
  • Proof of the debt: agreement, invoices, bank transfers, receipts, messages or emails
  • Your bank statement showing the cheque was presented

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

  • Notice sent after 30 days: no Section 138 case on that dishonour. If the cheque is still within its three-month validity, it can be presented again and the time limits start afresh from the new dishonour.
  • Complaint filed late: the court accepts it only if it is satisfied there was sufficient cause for the delay.
  • Either way, a civil suit to recover the money remains available, generally within three years.

Common questions

What if the person refuses to accept the legal notice?

If it was sent by registered or speed post to the correct address and returns as refused or unclaimed, courts generally treat it as served.

Can a cheque bounce case be settled?

Yes. The offence is compoundable, so the parties can settle at any stage, including during trial or appeal.

I have received a cheque bounce notice. What should I do?

Do not ignore it. Paying the cheque amount within 15 days of receiving the notice prevents the offence from arising. If you dispute the debt, reply in writing and keep a record.
