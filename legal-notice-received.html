Received a legal notice? What to do next

Jalaj Sharma, Advocate · November 2026 · 3 min read

In short

  1. Note the date you received it and keep the envelope.
  2. Do not ignore it, and do not reply in anger.
  3. Check whether a law attaches a deadline to it, such as 15 days in a cheque bounce.
  4. Reply in writing within the time given and keep proof of sending.

1. What is the legal problem?

You have received a letter from a lawyer on someone’s behalf, demanding money, asking you to do or stop doing something, or warning that a case will be filed.

2. What does the law say?

A legal notice is not a court order, and in most cases the law does not force you to reply. But some notices carry legal consequences:

  • Cheque bounce notice: paying within 15 days of receiving it prevents the offence under Section 138 from arising.
  • Tenancy notice: a notice ending a tenancy starts the period after which eviction proceedings can follow.
  • Notice before a suit: an unanswered notice can be used later to suggest you did not dispute the claim.

3. What should you do immediately?

  • Write down the date you received it and keep the envelope and tracking details.
  • Read the deadline it gives.
  • Do not admit anything, threaten or reply in anger, by message or by phone.
  • Gather the documents relating to the dispute.
  • Send a written reply within the time given, preferably through an advocate, by Speed Post or registered post.

4. What is the limitation period?

The deadline in a notice, often 7 to 30 days, is set by the sender and is not itself a law. The time limits that matter are the ones fixed by statute, such as 15 days in a cheque bounce notice. The limitation period for the sender to file a case runs on its own, whether or not you reply.

5. Which documents should you preserve?

  • The notice, the envelope and the tracking record
  • Any agreement, invoice, receipt or payment record related to the dispute
  • Messages and emails with the other side
  • A copy of your reply and proof of posting

6. What happens if you miss the deadline?

In a cheque bounce matter, not paying within 15 days completes the offence and a criminal complaint can follow. In other cases, the sender may file a suit or complaint. You can still defend yourself in court, but you lose the chance to put your version on record early.

This guide is general information on the law as it stands in November 2026. It is not legal advice; time limits and outcomes depend on the facts of each case.
